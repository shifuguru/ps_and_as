01 / The premise

Your finish follows you.

Presidents & Assholes is a shedding game where the finishing order follows you into the next deal. Empty your hand, then live with where you finished. The President gets the best card in the Asshole's new hand. Then the President decides what the Asshole gets back. Generosity is optional.

Watch the passes

Count what has been played. Notice who keeps passing. Then decide whether your good cards are worth spending.

Become President

Finish first and you become President. Finish last and you're the Asshole. The next deal knows the difference.

Hold Up

Sometimes your best move might be to pass.

02 / At the table

Play cards. Get out.

Lead a card or matching set, beat what is on the table, or pass. The aim is simple: shed your hand before the others do.

1

Lead

The first lead is the 3♣. After that, play a card or a matching set. Runs can help you shed more than one rank before the table catches up.

2

Beat or pass

Match the number of cards and beat the rank, or pass. Passing lets you keep a play back while everyone else shows what they have.

3

Change the next deal

Empty your hand first to become President. Then the President gets the advantage in the next deal; the player who finishes last has to hand over their best card.

05 / Rules desk

Know the ladder before you play.

Cards rise from 3 to 2, then Joker. Play singles or matching sets. Runs can let you shed ranks in sequence; 10s can send play higher or lower. A 2, Joker, or four-of-a-kind can turn a plain exchange into something else. The full rules explain the less obvious parts.

The useful surprise: The strongest cards are not a free pass. A Joker cannot join a run, a four-of-a-kind starts a challenge, and a 10 can change what “higher” means.

06 / First strategy notes

Things worth knowing early.

The full rules can wait. These are the habits that keep a decent hand from becoming a bad finish.

01

Keep an exit.
Sometimes the best card in your hand is the one you don't play.

02

Watch the passes.
A player who keeps passing may be protecting a pair, a 2, or something else they are waiting to complete.

03

Don't burn a 2 for nothing.
As the strongest normal rank, you may find you needed it as the table tightens.

04

Remember the next deal.
President takes the best card from Asshole. Someone has to be the Asshole; just don't let it be you.

07 / Questions before the deal

Good things to know.

How many players can play?
Local practice supports 3–8 seats. The rules also include special handling for two-player games, and five or more players add Vice President and Vice Asshole positions.
Can I play offline?
Yes. Local practice uses CPU opponents and does not require an online room once the game is loaded.
Can I play on mobile?
Yes. The browser game supports tap controls on mobile, and supported browsers can offer an installable web-app version.
Can I play online?
Yes. Create or join an online room and share its code with friends.
Is the game free?
Yes. The browser game is free to play.
Do I need every rule before I start?
No. Learn the basic play first. Open the full rules when a 10, run, Joker, or four-of-a-kind makes the obvious play less obvious.
